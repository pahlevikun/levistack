# Play it back

Use this in **brainstorm** mode, all the way through the conversation. Questions and play-backs take turns. A play-back shows the user what you understood, so they can fix it early.

## When to play back

As soon as you understand one part well enough to describe it, describe it. One part per play-back: what the thing is for and who it serves, or how one piece behaves. Then go back to questions about the next part. The last play-back covers whatever is left.

## How to write one

- Keep it to about 150 to 250 words.
- **Only what the user said goes in as theirs.** Anything you filled in goes in as your proposal, marked "my proposal", with your reason.
- End by asking what is wrong or missing.
- If the part is something people will look at (a page, a screen, a printout), and the user has not seen a sketch offer yet, send the offer first, in its own message. Hold the play-back for the next message.

## Shape

```
Here is what I have so far about <part>:

<the description, in plain words>

My proposals (not yours): <each fill-in and why>

What is wrong or missing?
```

## Notes

- A play-back is not approval of the whole design. Approval comes at the design brief.
- If the user corrects you, change the next play-back. Do not defend the first one.
