# English culture and transcreation

## Address and pronouns

| Situation | Form | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Internal Indo-US team | First name; `mas`/`kak` OK in mixed chat only | Not in external email |
| US work email | First name; direct ask | First sentence is the point |
| UK overlay | `please` / `shall we` sparingly | British spelling only when `uk` active |

## Directness

- Indo-US team: state the ask in sentence 1. Warmth from clarity, not filler.
- US: short paragraphs, concrete verbs (`send`, `fix`, `join`).
- UK: slightly softer openers OK; still ban corporate memo phrases.

## Humor and taboo

- Dry observation close is OK (the author fingerprint).
- Avoid sarcasm at someone's expense in MR review or incident text.

## Natural grammar

1. **SVO default** — subject before verb in work English; don't bury the ask.
2. **Articles** — `the API token` when definite; don't drop `the` in Indian English overlay without signal.
3. **Contractions** — OK in `santai`/`profesional` internal; fewer in `baku`.
4. **Preposition calques** — `in .env`, not `on .env` unless repo uses that phrase.
5. **Parallel lists** — same structure for bullets; no mixed gerunds and nouns.
6. **US spelling default** — `organize`, `color`; UK overlay switches spelling only.
7. **One intention per sentence** — split long chains; no corporate run-ons.

## Do not copy from English corporate

- `hope this email finds you well`, `kindly revert`, `as per`, `going forward`, `bandwidth` (for people), `leverage`, `synergy`, `robust`, `seamless`

## Dates and names

- Dates: `Tue 16 Sep, 14:00 WIB` or explicit timezone.
- US spelling default (`color`, `organize`) unless `regional_voice: uk` (or au/nz/ca/ie/za).
