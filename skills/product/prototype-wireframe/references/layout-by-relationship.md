# Choose the layout from the relationship

Use this for shape wireframes, flow maps and any diagram a prototype needs. Decide what the **relationship** is first. The look comes after.

## Say the point first

Before you draw, finish this line:

> After five seconds, the person should understand that ...

One picture gets one point. If you need two, make two pictures.

## Pick a family

| The information is | Family | Draw it as |
|---|---|---|
| Many steps or inputs narrowing to one choice | Convergence | Lanes that come together. The decision sits past the meeting point |
| A core idea with outer layers (why, how, what) | Concentric | Shapes that share a center. Labels sit on their own ring |
| Levels, scope or containment | Layers | Nested or stacked bands whose size shows scope |
| Two or three groups that share something | Overlap | Two or three fields. Label the unique parts and the shared part |
| Before and after, or old and new | Side by side | Same scale, same frame. Mark the difference |
| A measured trend | Annotated chart | One chart, direct labels, one callout, a source line |
| Real product behavior | Real screenshot | A genuine capture with one to three callouts |
| Screens and moves between them | Flow map | Frames and arrows (see `wireframe.md`) |

Then finish the matching line. If the line is weak, the family is wrong.

- "This is a **convergence** because ..."
- "These are **layers** because ..."
- "The **overlap** matters because ..."
- "The comparison is fair because both sides share ..."

## Honest content

- Use real content. If you do not have it, use a fixture and label it "sample data" on the page.
- A drawn number equals what is on screen. A chart has a scale and a unit. A side-by-side uses the same scale on both sides.
- Never draw a fake product screen to prove a claim. Capture the real one.
- Never invent a quote, a statistic or a source.

## Keep the layout varied across a set

When one prototype has four or more diagrams or wireframes:

- Use at least three different families.
- No more than two share the same main composition.
- Change density and scale from one to the next.
- At least one has no grid of cards.

## Avoid

- A headline plus three rounded cards for every idea.
- A dark dashboard ground for every topic.
- An icon beside every bullet only as decoration.
- Everything centered.
- A picture that means nothing once its title is covered.
