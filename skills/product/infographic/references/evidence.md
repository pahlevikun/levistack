# Evidence and claim boundaries

Keep source observations, calculations and editorial interpretation distinct. A polished mark should not give weak evidence more authority than it has.

## Research pack

Read every supplied file or page relevant to the claim. Record inaccessible sources and what they block. Use primary sources for factual detail when available. Date time-sensitive material and preserve dataset version, selection criteria and capture context.

For each claim or asset, record its source locator, status, transformation, uncertainty and reuse limits. Use measured for your own reproducible observation, sourced for an identified statement or dataset, inferred for your interpretation, unknown for an unresolved fact and synthetic for a clearly labeled example.

Build only the evidence structure the task needs. A short graphic may need two claims and a source line. A research page may need lanes for observation, attestation, variant, context, uncertainty and rights. Do not force every project into six columns.

## Claims to check

- State whose population, place and time the data covers. A sample is not the whole population.
- State how missing, suppressed, restricted and pending records differ. Missing data does not prove absence.
- Keep frequency separate from importance. Attention, usage, cultural value and causal impact are different claims.
- A first recorded attestation is not necessarily the first historical occurrence. OCR quality, archive coverage and variant spelling affect the result.
- Define curated categories and semantic groupings. They are editorial choices unless an authoritative source defines them.
- Describe associations without adding causation. Do not imply legal, clinical or policy meaning that the sources do not establish.
- Keep direct quotes exact and short, with their context. Do not create an attributed quote from a paraphrase.
- Use a real screenshot for a product claim. Preserve capture date, state and source. Do not invent interface details in an illustration.

Put the relevant caveat beside the mark. A footnote cannot repair a misleading headline or an unlabeled scale.

## Transform ledger

Record raw inputs, filters, exclusions, aggregation, normalization, rounding and visual encoding. Keep raw and derived values separately. Explain any index, rank, log scale, square-root area or curated score. Preserve enough input to recompute the result.

For percentages, name the denominator. For rate changes, distinguish percentage points from relative change. For amounts, state currency, price basis and date when they matter. Check totals after rounding and explain any mismatch.

## Rights and attribution

Credit factual sources separately from design references. A method learned from a design reference does not grant rights to its datasets, imagery, brand or finished layout. Keep licenses and restrictions attached to reused assets. Summarize restricted material and use only permitted excerpts.

Study a reference's hierarchy, comparison method and spatial logic. Build a new composition for this content. Do not copy distinctive art or identity. If the user wants a historical influence, name the actual source and the useful principle. A country or design school label is not a substitute for evidence.

Retain source URLs and attribution in the editable source and export where needed. Mark unresolved rights before release. Do not publish, license or upload a source as part of a local graphic request.
